Conference paper: Quantum Computing in Financial Services

Abstract

This paper analyses the future of quantum computing and its application in the world of financial services and how this technology could allow financial institutions to evolve.

Introduction

Standard Classical Computing models use binary bits of either value 0 or 1 (either on or off). Whilst in small numbers binary bits cannot display a lot of information, eg 4 bits can represent 16 different pieces of information. The formula for this is N=2^n. Classical computers are simply systems of billions of these bits set up to create logic gates which can be used to perform calculations. This Binary system powers almost all software in today's world. While Quantum computers use Qubits which differ from Binary bits in the sense that instead of only being on or off, Qubits can be in a superposition of either "on or off" until observed.

Analysis and discussion

Why is it Mathematically relevant?

What this means is that while a string of Binary bits can only represent 1 string given by formula N=2^n, a string of Qubits of the same length can represent all of the possible strings that the same number of binary bits could represent at once until observed. Whilst this is a much larger mathematical space, it does not automatically mean quantum computing is superior to the binary bit system. This is because complex algorithms need to be written to decode these quantum strings to receive a result.

As shown in the graph, both binary bits and qubits have 2 to the power of n possible basis states. However, qubits can exist in a superposition of these states, which is one of the features that makes quantum computing different from classical computing.



Why does quantum computing not run modern software right now?

Whilst multiple hardware platforms exist in 2026, the technology to support their hardware is still developing. This means in 2026 using quantum computers can still not be used for RSA decryption. One single Qubit that gives a logical output has many qubits that aren't logical, meaning large swathes of hardware are needed. This means quantum computers cannot yet be practical for everyday use.

Connectivity is also a problem as Qubits and binary systems operate differently and cannot directly interact without specialised interfaces. Algorithm limitations also prevent Quantum computers from being able to solve all large-scale mathematical problems attributed with financial datasets.

Why Does Quantum computing change security in financial services?

Cryptography (Cryptography is the science, study, and practice of securing and authenticating people, data, transactions) is important in financial services. RSA cryptography is one of the most common forms of security during online transactions and works via a form of prime factorisation.

Conventional binary computers could in theory be used to find the prime factors of these huge numbers, allowing for criminals to find private keys giving access to multiple different facets of online wallets as well as private online banking.

However Financial services will also benefit from this because of the number of mathematical calculations being possible.

Investment Banking – Derivatives Pricing

One Real world area where this could happen is in investment banking specifically in Derivatives pricing, where banks price financial instruments such as: Mortgage-backed securities or interest rate swaps.

Pricing these requires an immense amount of computation power as Highly sophisticated mathematical models are used. These models begin to produce errors = O(1/√N).

This can be reduced by Quantum amplitude estimation to O(1/N), essentially meaning an improved evaluation of potential risks and potential price and upsides of complex products.

Retail and Commercial Banking

Retail and commercial banking will also be impacted in two main ways.

Credit Risk Assessment

The first being through Credit risk assessment. This is where banks take large sets of data about people and their financial habits and determine the likelihood that any credit borrowed, whether through credit cards or through mortgages, will be defaulted on or paid back.

Models predicting this use 10's of variables across millions of people and need to identify patterns in these complex datasets. However conventional computing power struggles to keep up while quantum machine learning could potentially help identify patterns in these financial datasets.

Fraud Detection

Another key way is because of Quantum machine learning and applies to Fraud detection. This is because often time financial fraud uses sophisticated networks of transactions, accounts and people to commit fraud which could potentially be easier to decipher and identify with quantum machine learning.

How Does RSA Encryption Work?

The way it works in the modern world is through Prime Factorisation, also referred to as RSA encryption. This works by taking the two prime factors of a number, eg 91 has the prime factors 7 and 13.

In this example, 91 would be a simplified example of a publicly known number. The difficulty comes when numbers become extremely large, as conventional computers struggle to work backwards and find the original prime factors.

Conclusion

Ultimately, quantum computing could have a significant impact on financial services because of its ability to approach certain mathematical problems differently from conventional binary computers.

This could affect financial security through RSA encryption, while also creating opportunities for investment banking through derivatives pricing and risk assessment. Retail and commercial banking could also benefit from potential improvements in credit risk assessment and fraud detection.

Although quantum computing is still developing and cannot currently replace conventional computing systems, its mathematical applications could become increasingly important to the financial services industry.
